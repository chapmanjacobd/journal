The one thing I wish improved was scripting

It's likely worth learning a bit of R or Python directly.

I love spreadsheets but once I can work through the problem by playing around with the numbers in a spreadsheet, I definitely prefer to keep scripting separate as it's easier to audit all the formulae are being applied correctly rather than being hidden inside a cell.

Useful R packages to learn: data.table, openxlsx2, stringr, lubridate, and then whatever domain specific needs you might have like caret or jfa.

The interactivity of RStudio almost reaches the level of a spreadsheet depending on the problem that you're working on.

data.table is such good design I'd consider it one of the best designed technologies in the world--perhaps even better than SQL. The syntax is pretty terse though so it helps to have the manual open to cross reference as you're getting comfortable with it.
