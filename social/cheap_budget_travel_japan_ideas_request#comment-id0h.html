Well the main reason I suggest is that you want 'Japan but cheap' and you can have a very similar experience to Japan for about half the cost or even less (maybe less true for the extreme budget traveler while the Yen is so low but this is still definitely the case for mid-tier and luxury travel).

Many people just go to Japan and don't visit other cultures which is a real shame because they are missing out--especially when people pay so much for a ticket to Japan and then a round-trip ticket between Japan and Korea is often less than $75. It's often cheaper overall to fly into Japan or Korea and then fly out of the other country vs the cost of a round-trip to the same country (this really depends on specific airline pricing but I would say it is true >50% of the time: https://share.gemini.google/Hlb2BzWHQki8)

Korea is very accessible and easy to travel around with just Google Maps and Rome2Rio. It's not like China where everything is locked down. I don't know any Korean and people don't really expect you to if you're a foreigner.

Actual lived culture is also very different from what they externally portray in Korean TV dramas and other media so if you skim WikiVoyage honestly you'll know enough to get a lot out of it. Most signs and museum materials are also in multiple languages (they get a lot of Chinese tourists so most places with 3+ employees will usually have someone onsite who can speak Chinese and someone who can speak English)
